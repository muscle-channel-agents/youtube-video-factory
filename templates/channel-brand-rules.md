## Script Rules

- Start with a line that can stand alone.
- Do not begin with explanation.
- Keep each video centered on one transformation.
- Use direct second-person language.
- Use short English lines for key moments.
- Let the Japanese subtitle carry nuance.
- End with one concrete mission.

## Visual Rules

- Every video needs at least three symbolic inserts.
- Every video needs at least one human close-up.
- Every video needs at least one action cut.
- Every video needs at least one quiet text cut.
- Avoid random B-roll that only looks cool.
- Avoid visuals that imply a specific copyrighted film, athlete, actor, or creator.

## Thumbnail Rules

- Use 1-4 English words.
- Use simple English Japanese viewers can understand.
- Show one strong emotion.
- Prefer close-up faces, lone figures, or symbolic roads.
- Avoid crowded thumbnails.
- Avoid small Japanese text.

## Subtitle Rules

- Japanese subtitles are burned in.
- English key phrases appear larger than Japanese subtitles.
- Japanese subtitles should be natural, compact, and emotional.
- Do not translate slang literally if it sounds awkward.
- Keep text inside mobile safe areas.

## Audio Rules

- Voice should be consistent across videos.
- Vary performance by chapter, not by randomly changing voices.
- BGM supports the speech and should not become the content.
- Use silence before important final lines.
- Sound effects should be subtle.

## Originality Rules

- The video must answer: why does this exist as its own piece?
- The script must have a thesis, not just quotes.
- The scene board must show cause and effect.
- The final mission must match the video theme.
- The publish description must state the original production process.

## Review Questions

- Would this still work without famous people?
- Does the video have a point of view?
- Are the cinematic inserts metaphors, not decoration?
- Can a viewer describe this video in one sentence?
- Does the thumbnail promise match the final feeling?
